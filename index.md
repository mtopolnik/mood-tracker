# Privacy Policy for Cloudy

**Last updated: May 16, 2026**

Cloudy is an Android app for keeping a private daily mood questionnaire.
This policy explains exactly what happens to your data. The short version:
**Cloudy has no servers and no account, collects nothing, and sends nothing
anywhere. Your data stays on your device.**

Data controller: **Marko Topolnik**
Contact: **marko.topolnik@gmail.com**

## The data Cloudy handles

When you use Cloudy you enter answers to a daily 12‑item questionnaire. From
those answers the app derives two category scores (an *anxiety* score and a
*depression* score) and the date of each entry. This information relates to
your mental wellbeing and is therefore **sensitive, health‑related
information**. It is stored in a database in Cloudy's own private storage area
on your device.

Cloudy does **not** ask for or store your name, email, account, device
identifiers, location, contacts, or any advertising identifier.

## What Cloudy does with it

Your entries are used **only on your device**, to:

- show and let you edit today's questionnaire and the last 30 days,
- compute the live category scores and the trends chart,
- show an optional daily reminder notification.

That is the entire purpose. The data is never used for profiling,
advertising, or analytics.

## What Cloudy does *not* do

- **No collection by the developer.** Marko Topolnik and Cloudy never receive,
  see, or store any of your data.
- **No network access.** The app does not request the Android `INTERNET`
  permission. It is technically incapable of transmitting your data off the
  device on its own.
- **No analytics, crash reporting, advertising, or tracking.** Cloudy includes
  no third‑party SDKs that send data anywhere. There are no cookies and no
  trackers.
- **No data selling or sharing.** There is nothing to sell or share, because
  nothing is collected.

## Notifications

If you enable the daily reminder, Cloudy uses the Android notification
permission (`POST_NOTIFICATIONS`) to show a local reminder generated entirely
on your device. There is no push server and no message is sent or received
over the network.

## Data that can leave your device — only by your choice or your own Google account

Cloudy itself never transmits data. Data can leave the device only in these
two ways, both under your control:

- **Android Auto Backup.** If device backup is enabled, Android may copy
  Cloudy's data to *your own* Google account backup storage so it can be
  restored when you set up a new device with the same account. This backup is
  performed by Android/Google, not by Cloudy, and is governed by
  [Google's Privacy Policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy). You can
  control or disable it, and delete existing backups, in your device's backup
  settings and your Google account.
- **Manual export.** You can export your entries to a file and send it, using
  the Android share sheet, to a destination *you* pick (for example Google
  Drive, email, or local files). Once you export or share that file, it is
  under your control and subject to the privacy policy of wherever you sent
  it. Importing reads a file you select; the imported data stays on your
  device.

## Retention and deletion

Your data stays on your device until you remove it:

- **Uninstalling Cloudy deletes its on‑device database.** (Cloudy has no
  in‑app "delete everything" button; uninstalling is the way to erase the
  local data.)
- Any **export files** you created exist wherever you saved or sent them; you
  delete those yourself.
- Any **Auto Backup** copy is managed through your device's backup settings
  and your Google account, where you can delete it.

## Your rights

Because the developer holds none of your data, there is no remote profile to
access, correct, export, or erase. You already have full, direct control on
the device: you can review and edit every entry, export your data at any time,
and erase the local data by uninstalling the app. If you have any questions
about this policy or your data, contact **marko.topolnik@gmail.com**.

## Children

Cloudy is not directed to children and does not knowingly collect data from
anyone — it collects no personal data at all.

## Security

Your entries are kept in Cloudy's private app storage, protected by Android's
application sandbox and your device's own security (such as your lock screen
and device encryption). Cloudy does not add a separate encryption layer beyond
these operating‑system protections, so keeping your device secured with a
screen lock is recommended.

## Not medical advice

Cloudy is a personal self‑tracking tool. It is not a medical device and does
not provide diagnosis, treatment, or professional advice. If you are concerned
about your mental health, please consult a qualified professional.

## Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the updated version will be published at this page and
the "Last updated" date above will change. Material changes will be reflected
here before they take effect in a new app release.
